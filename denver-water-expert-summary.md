# Denver Water Context (Cassidy Rosenkrantz)

- Colorado’s oldest/largest drinking water utility, est. 1918
  - Serves 1.5M people, ~25% of state population
  - Collection system spans 4,000 sq miles; distribution system 3,000 miles of pipeline
  - Owns and operates 9 reservoirs across two systems: north (20% supply) and south (80% supply)
- 2026 is one of the worst droughts on record
  - Lowest snowpack and warmest year on record; 90-degree day in March
  - Peak runoff hit end of March vs. typical May/June
  - Forced early reservoir draining and use of lower-quality alternative sources
- Key challenges: water quantity scarcity, wildfire/flood water quality impacts, tightening regulation
  - PFAS (“forever compounds”) regulation starts 2027
  - Some treatment plants are 100 years old, making compliance costly and complex
- Emerging opportunities: Starlink enabling remote sensors, shift away from manual Excel-based data analysis

  3. Big-picture system visualization: model how hydrological events move through the whole system, comparing historical, drought, and wet-year scenarios
