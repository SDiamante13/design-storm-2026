# **Biggest Pain Point: Visualization and Digital Twin**

- No visualization layer on current water data
- Ideal state: a digital twin that models all constituents, integrates external datasets, and tells the story of different conditions across the org
- Current monitoring is purely real-time or historical; no forward-looking predictive model at the point level
- Goal: start modeling conditions ahead of time to anticipate issues

# **Data and Predictive Modeling**

- Interpolating missing water data is an open question: what gaps exist and can they be filled?
- SNOTEL dataset in use; stations identified by number, scattered geographically
  - Started with Michigan Creek station, found data anomalies, switched to Buckskin Joe
- Weather/radar data (e.g. NEXRAD) used only for big-picture water quantity estimates
  - Inputs: historical datasets, real-time streamflow, snowpack, snow water equivalent
  - Not used for acute, point-level stream analysis

# **Political and Organizational Challenges**

- Water rights: legal obligation to use allocated water but also to release water downstream
  - Tension in drought years between serving constituents and not harming downstream cities
- Public perception: drought restrictions on residents while utility flushes hydrants for water quality
  - Flushing is necessary to prevent quality issues, but generates public complaints
- Parks and public spaces may operate under different watering rules than individual users
- Competing stakeholders: recreation (rafting), fisheries (need flow and temperature), and drinking water demand all pull in different directions

# **Treatment Plant Operations**

- Filtration replacement schedule based on pathogen risk
  - Risk factor calculated at start of treatment process
  - Requires minimum filter time and disinfection residual
- Treatment chemicals are a major cost: hundreds of millions of dollars
- Three distinct audiences for any visualization tool:
  - Water quantity team (50 people): how much water, how to move it, water rights
  - Watershed scientists (Steven’s team): water quality, regulation, environmental stewardship
  - Treatment plants: incoming water quality, treatment efficiency, cost minimization

# **Case Study: 2023 South Platte 100-Year Storm**

- A 100-year storm hit the South Platte Watershed
- Scale was not anticipated, even though the storm was known to be coming
- Turbidity and water quality overwhelmed the treatment plant filters
  - Nearly forced a plant shutdown
  - Managed with chemicals, but filters had to be taken offline for major cleaning
  - Significantly slowed the rate of water entering the system
- Served as a key trigger: recognized this type of event needs to be modeled and anticipated in advance
