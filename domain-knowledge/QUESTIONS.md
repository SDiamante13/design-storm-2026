# Questions for Denver Water

Open questions for Cassidi Rosenkrance and Jake Slawson. Take them to the 4:45 Q&A
on Sep 24 or ask during the morning on Sep 25. Record answers here with who answered
and when; don't paraphrase a guess as an answer.

Context: [problem-frame.md](problem-frame.md).

## 1. Which sources fed Foothills in 2026 compared with 2024?

**Why we ask.** Foothills water looked very different in 2026: alkalinity was below
60 mg/L on 23% of Apr to Aug days, compared with 64% in 2024, and TOC never went above
4 mg/L. Cassidi said that in 2026 Denver Water drained reservoirs and "pulled in
alternative sources." If Foothills drew a different mix of sources, the quality change
may come from that decision, not from the snowpack. The repo has no data on which
sources were used.

**What changes with the answer.** If the sources were the same, we can present the
snow-to-quality link with more confidence. If they differ, source choice becomes part
of the model or a limit we state.

**Answer:** _open_

## 2. What do your planners call dry, average and wet years, and how do they define them?

**Why we ask.** Cassidi said the planners model "a wet year, a dry year, and an
average year." Our draft calls this **year type**, a term we made up. We don't know
Denver Water's word for it, how many categories there are, or what defines each one
(snowpack, streamflow, percent of median, a percentile).

**What changes with the answer.** We use their term and their cut-offs, so our
quality view lines up with the scenarios they already run. Until then we label any
stand-in as ours.

**Answer:** _open_

## 3. Which gage best shows natural runoff into the South system?

**Why we ask.** Our flow record, DWR PLASPLCO (South Platte at South Platte), sits
below Antero, Eleven Mile and Cheesman reservoirs, so its flow mostly shows how those
reservoirs were released. Its 2026 peak was Jul 18, not in late March when Cassidi
said runoff peaked.

**Answer:** _open_

**Why we ask.** All the plant-intake quality data we have is from Foothills.

**Answer:** _open_

## 5. Who would use a "days below 60 alkalinity per year type" view: planners, plants, or both?

**Why we ask.** It decides how we frame the report-out and what the model shows first.

**Answer:** _partial._ Cassidi, Sep 24 Q&A, on visualization in general (not this view
